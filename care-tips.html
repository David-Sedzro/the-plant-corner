Plant Care Tips

Care for different kinds of plants

Use these tips as a starting point, and adjust to your plant, soil, and the season.

Houseplants

Most houseplants prefer bright, indirect light. Keep them out of harsh afternoon sun, which can scorch leaves.

As a rough guide, check the soil every 5–7 days and water when the top inch (about 2–3 cm) feels dry. Water thoroughly, let excess drain, and empty the saucer so the roots do not sit in water. Adjust the timing to the plant and weather.

Succulents & cacti

These plants prefer bright light and can often handle direct sun. Introduce stronger sunlight gradually to reduce the chance of leaf scorch.

They store water in their leaves and stems, so let the soil dry completely before watering again. Every 2–3 weeks can be a starting point in warm, bright conditions, but check the soil rather than relying on a fixed schedule. Use a fast-draining mix and a pot with drainage holes.

Tropical plants

Keep tropical plants in a warm spot with bright, indirect light. Avoid cold drafts and direct airflow from air-conditioning vents.

Many enjoy higher humidity. Group plants together or place a tray of water nearby; occasional gentle misting can help, but avoid leaving leaves constantly wet.

Outdoor plants

During the dry season, check outdoor pots and beds regularly. Water early in the morning or later in the evening to reduce water loss from heat. Water the soil deeply, while making sure containers can drain freely.

During the growing season, a general-purpose fertilizer every 4–6 weeks may help. Follow the product label and avoid fertilizing dry or stressed plants.
