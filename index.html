Frequently Asked Questions

Simple answers to common plant care questions.

How often should I water my plants?

Check the soil before you water. For many houseplants, wait until the top 2–3 cm feels dry, then water slowly until a little drains from the bottom. Empty the saucer afterward so the roots don't sit in water.

Warmth, pot size, and the season all affect how quickly soil dries, so use the plant and soil as your guide rather than following a fixed calendar.

How much sunlight does my plant need?

Many popular indoor plants prefer bright, indirect light: near a bright window, but out of harsh direct afternoon sun. Low-light plants still need some natural light; “low light” doesn't mean no light.

Watch how your plant responds. Pale, scorched patches can mean too much direct sun, while slow, stretched growth can mean it needs a brighter spot.

What soil should I use, and when should I repot?

Choose a pot with drainage holes and a potting mix suited to your plant. A general indoor mix works for many houseplants; cacti and succulents need a faster-draining blend.

Repot when roots are crowded, growing through drainage holes, or the soil dries unusually fast. Move up just one pot size to avoid leaving too much wet soil around the roots.

Why are my plant's leaves yellow, drooping, or spotted?

These signs can have several causes. Start with a simple check:

  • Feel the soil and check that the pot drains freely.
  • Look for recent changes in light, temperature, or watering.
  • Check both sides of the leaves for pests or unusual marks.

One older yellow leaf can be normal. If several leaves change at once or you spot pests, isolate the plant and ask Ama what to do next.
